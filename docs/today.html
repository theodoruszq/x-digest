Patrick Collison: if AI makes human labor redundant, do we still get the next Mozart and Dickens?

@patrickc·♥Likes: 784·Original·Nov 15, 01:04 UTC

Smith and Montesquieu thought that markets and commerce made us better: that they create an incentive to pursue self-improvement and to create things of value for others. In the positive AI visions, where the labor of humans is made redundantadj. 多余的, I find myself wondering how we fare in the presumptive absence of these incentive gradients激励梯度（推动人努力的动力差）. …

both Mozart and Dickens were themselves deeply commercially-motivated in the production of their work, as were most of the great artists through human history. If the premise is that nobody will need to work or produce, how confident are we that we actually get the next Mozart and Dickens? The relevant question is not the partial equilibrium局部均衡（只看单个人/单个市场） of whether one individual can pursue a life of enlightened leisure, but the general equilibrium一般均衡（看整个社会的整体结果） of what form a society as a whole will take on.

💡 Stripe 联合创始人的长文思考：莫扎特和狄更斯都很看重挣钱，商业激励本身催生了伟大作品。若 AI 让人人不必工作，“个人能悠闲搞艺术”不等于“整个社会还能产出大师”。
